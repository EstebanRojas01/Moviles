## Interview 2 — Juan Jose Murillo Aristizabal — Free Time Matching at U
> Video recording: [Interview](./Free%20Time%20Matching%20at%20U%20Interview.mp4)


| # | Question | Purpose |
|---|----------|---------|
| 1 | How many courses are you taking this semester, and do you live near campus? | Understand the student's context and how much time they spend on campus |
| 2 | How do you usually spend your free time between classes? | Identify current habits and routines during downtime |
| 3 | How do you usually make plans with friends at the university? | Understand current coordination behavior and channels used |
| 4 | Walk me through the last time you tried to coordinate something with a group, what happened? | Elicit a specific situation (critical incident, What-Why-How) |
| 5 | Have you ever skipped doing something because coordinating felt like too much effort? | Measure friction pain point and how often it blocks social plans |
| 6 | What tools do you currently use to coordinate with others?  | Benchmark existing tools and workarounds |
| 7 | How often do you feel like you have free time but don't know who else is available? | Validate the core hypothesis — frequency of the problem |
| 8 | How important is it to you to meet up spontaneously vs. planning ahead? | Understand user preference and openness to real-time coordination |
| 9 | Have you ever wished there was an easier way to know when your friends are available on campus? | Gauge awareness and desire for a solution before presenting the concept |
| 10 | Imagine an app where students can mark when they're free on campus and instantly see who else is free nearby. What's your first reaction? | Elicit ideal solution / initial emotional response to the concept |
| 11 | Would you use it? Why or why not? | Assess adoption intent and uncover potential barriers |
| 12 | What would give you confidence to use it, and what would make you distrust it? | Identify trust factors and deal-breakers |
| 13 | What's the one thing that would make this app work or fail for you? | Identify the single most critical feature or risk |
